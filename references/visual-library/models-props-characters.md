# Models, props, buildings, vehicles, creatures and characters reference library

What this is: 36 checked references (39 rows; three point at rows in the environments file) for single assets and asset families in styles that suit Roblox, including the weak and generated examples that show what to avoid. The rules they support live in `references/blender-modeling-craft.md` (numbers, recipes, per-asset rubric) and `references/art-direction-and-quality-bar.md` (slop tells, style choice, AI line).

How to use this file:
- Before modelling an asset class, open one strong row, one Weak row and one "too realistic" row, and put all three on the asset's board with a label each (on target, too generic, too realistic). View each at 64–96 px tall: that is the size it will have in play.
- Evidence: `viewed` = the image was downloaded and looked at; `read` = the page or thread was read; `oembed` = video verified, not watched; `search` = seen in search results only, not opened.
- These are references to study, not assets to copy. Face counts are as posted by the author and are often far above a Roblox budget; licences are recorded so a model can be opened and measured, never shipped (house rule 1: everything visible is our own Blender work).

Image host prefixes: `DF` = `https://devforum-uploads.s3.dualstack.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/original`, `LV` = `https://cdn.80.lv/api/upload/content`, `GD` = `https://eu-images.contentstack.com/v3/assets/blt740a130ae3c5d529`. Sketchfab rows link the model page; its preview is at `https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/models/<uid>` under `thumbnails`.

## Patterns seen

- **One oversized feature, everything else quiet**: the mouth (20), the shoulders and hammer (28), the hands and feet (23), the tall shard (3). Exaggeration came in pairs: big hands on thin limbs.
- **Two or three colours per asset, one accent**: red and cream (20), steel and maroon (29), white with one accent (31), warm against cool (4). Gradients replaced texture detail (3, 23).
- **A motif repeated across the asset** makes it read as designed: the knot on three armour pieces (28), folded paper on every prop (6).
- **Family before colour**: a shared chamfer made four grey props a set (1); the bevelled cube shows why (8).
- **Budgets that fit Roblox exist in good work**: 790 faces (3), 1,003 (7), 1,082 for a rock set (2), 3,434 (30), 4,331 (23), 6,094 (14), 6,976 (28).
- **Boards were sorted by question or by source**, never one pile (12, 21), and blockouts were colour-blocked beside the concept (5).

## What the weak examples had in common

- **One source copied**: the cube pet (25, 26, 27). Recolour as "new content"; identity carried by a decal instead of the shape.
- **Real-world proportion kept**: thin, narrow, small-wheeled (16); the realistic van (17) shows the same proportions need full PBR to work, which a small team cannot sustain.
- **Detail without decisions**: the generated set (34–39): even density, even wear, baked light, pattern on every surface, undecided forms. Nothing was chosen, so nothing leads.
- **No bevel, no hierarchy, no palette**: untextured grey boxes (16) and stock templates fail the first three lines of the asset rubric before detail is even discussed.
