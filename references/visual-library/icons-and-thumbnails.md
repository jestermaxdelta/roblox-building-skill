# Icons, thumbnails and store-page reference library

What this is: 76 checked references for the game icon, the 16:9 thumbnails, the gameplay video and the tools that make them: live tiles of shipped Roblox games, artist portfolios, critique threads, official examples, tutorials and specs. The rules these references support live in `references/icons-thumbnails-and-store-page.md` (recipes in §3, tells in §11, rubric in §15).

How to use this file:
- Pick the recipe or genre you are making, open three rows, and write down what you will copy as a layout decision (subject size, value split, text or none) before opening Figma or Blender. Shrink every image you study to 160 px wide: what survives is the lesson.
- Evidence: `viewed` = the image was downloaded and looked at (live tiles at full size and reduced to 150, 64 and 32 px); `read` = the page or thread was read; `oembed` = the video exists with that title and creator, it was not watched; `search` = seen only in search results, not opened.
- These are references to study, not assets to copy: every third-party image stays its owner's. Live tiles change with every update; rows describe what was live on 2026-11-11.

Fetching a live tile: game rows give the place link and the universe id (`u…`). Icon: `https://thumbnails.roblox.com/v1/games/icons?universeIds=<u>&size=512x512&format=Png`. Thumbnails: `https://thumbnails.roblox.com/v1/games/multiget/thumbnails?universeIds=<u>&countPerUniverse=5&size=768x432&format=Png`. Both return JSON with an `imageUrl`. Image host prefixes: `DF` = `https://devforum-uploads.s3.dualstack.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads`, `DOCS` = `https://prod.docsiteassets.roblox.com/assets/publishing/experience-metadata`.

## Patterns seen

Across 62 live icons, about 155 live thumbnails and about 55 forum and docs images:

- **One subject at the right size.** Strong icons fill 60–80% of the square with one shape; strong thumbnails give the hero 30–45% and one second element. Everything that survived 32 px had three or fewer value masses.
- **A fixed system, a changing scene.** The long-lived games keep a frame, sign, wordmark corner or character treatment constant (rows 12, 28–31, 41, 42) and swap what is inside.
- **The promise is a verb.** The clearest tiles show the action in progress: throwing, cutting, packing, digging, hooking (rows 7, 22, 27, 36, 39). Hands in frame are the cheapest way to say "you do this".
- **The climb in one frame.** Splits and three-panel ladders with one word or number per panel read at 160×90 (rows 16, 23, 24, 40).
- **Genre is carried by value.** Horror medians near 0.19 luminance, roleplay near 0.62; the standouts change exactly one norm (rows 2, 4, 44).
- **Diagonals and scale.** Action tiles tilt the main line 15–30°; spectacle tiles pair a tiny avatar with a subject four or more times its size (rows 19, 39, 42, 43, 46).
- **No text is common.** 35 of 62 icons carried no text; among text users the wordmark is the usual choice, and only one or two heavy words survive 64 px.
- **In-engine honesty can win.** Several top games show their real blocks, lanes and UI (rows 18, 19, 26, 28); two staff-cited games gained 30–41% RFY plays from real gameplay video (rows 62, 63).

## What the weak examples had in common

- **No promise.** Abstract scenes, meme captions or "can you survive?" in place of what the player does (row 52); a collage of everything (row 21).
- **Too dark or too even.** The subject shares the background's value, so nothing reads at tile size (rows 37, 54, 56).
- **Tiny subject.** A wide beauty shot with the hero under 10% of the frame, or a raw HUD screenshot (rows 37, 45).
- **Text that cannot survive.** Sentence-length captions, long numbers with commas, and text-only icons (row 13).
- **A finish that is not the game's.** Glossy plastic avatars, borrowed props, painterly or photoreal objects beside blocky characters, a different avatar in each slot (rows 27, 49, 53, 68).
- **A baked-in date.** "UPDATE!" ribbons and seasonal art left up after the season (row 11; 14 of 98 chart titles still carried Halloween marks on 11 November).
- **Policy-blind detail.** Blood on an otherwise excellent image was enough for a rejection (row 55).
