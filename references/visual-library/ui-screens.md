# UI reference library: screens

What this is: 77 checked references for the windows a Roblox game needs: shops, inventories, trading, daily rewards and passes, menus, settings, codes, dialogue, loading, reward popups and notifications, from hit Roblox games, commissioned Roblox work and shipped games outside Roblox.
The rules these references support live in `references/ui-screen-recipes.md` (anatomy, sizes, server rules per screen), `references/ui-design-figma.md` §8 (generic tells) and `references/monetization-and-policy.md` (what a shop may and may not say).

How to use this file:
- Open the section for the screen you are building, pick three rows, and for each write the anatomy top to bottom (header, tabs, body, footer) and the one thing the header tells the player before you open Figma.
- Evidence: `viewed` = the image was downloaded and looked at; `read` = the page or thread was read; `oembed` = video verified, not watched; `search` = seen in search results only. `†` after the evidence word = the link is a Fandom `File:` page: it opens in a browser, but Fandom answers HTTP 403 to automated clients (curl, fetch tools), so an agent should download the `WK/…` image path given in the same row instead. Each such file was confirmed to exist through its wiki's `api.php` on 2026-11-11. Row numbers are stable ids that the guides cite: a removed row leaves a gap. Every link and image path was re-requested on 2026-11-11: all answered.
- These are references to study, not assets to copy: every screenshot and kit stays its owner's. Several hit-game shops show discount tags, struck-through prices or stock counters; the notes flag them, and house rule 3 decides what you may use. Accessed 2026-11-11.

Image host prefixes: `DF` = `https://devforum-uploads.s3.dualstack.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/uploads/original`, `WK` = `https://static.wikia.nocookie.net`, `IIG` = `https://interfaceingame.com/wp-content/uploads`, `DOCS` = `https://prod.docsiteassets.roblox.com/assets`. "megathread" = DevForum thread t/311776 "Show off your UI designs" (the link goes to the post).

## Patterns seen across these references

- The header carries identity plus the one live fact of that screen (restock timer, balance, capacity, season end) and the close button; nothing else (#1, #8, #35, #38, #5).
- A price is a button, in a colour reserved for that currency; cards that sell for two currencies show two buttons side by side (#2, #12, #10, #8).
- Currency packs grow their art with the tier, and the top tier gets a larger card rather than a louder colour (#10, #9, #5, #13).
- Tabs are icon + word, at most six, often colour-coded by category (#6, #8, #9, #24).
- Rarity is a word chip or a star count on top of the colour, never colour alone (#1, #8, #24, #70).
- Rotating stock, passes and events state a real remaining time as a duration (#1, #7, #17, #18, #38, #42).
- Progress is written as have/need next to the bar: discovered, owned, capacity, tier XP (#23, #40, #36, #24, #17).
- Passes use the same anatomy everywhere: a numbered rail, the free row on one side, the paid row with padlocks on the other, the end date in the header (#36, #37, #38, #39).
- Rewards arrive as one summary that shows counts or the maths, with one exit (#67, #70, #72, #68, #69).
- Locked and empty states say why and what to do (#27, #53, #54, #8, #23).
- The strongest shops take their materials from the game's world: studs for a farm, maroon and a condensed serif for horror, a boutique window for fashion, a toy phone for trading (#1, #4, #7, #33).
- Professional mockups are filled with the longest strings and largest numbers, and one kit (header, X, ribbon) is reused across every window (#11, #56, #12); structure is settled in boxes before style (#76, #77).

## What the weak examples had in common

Drawn from captures rejected while building this file: megathread posts 5, 163, 466 and 1274; threads t/4859702, t/4967057, t/4706951, t/4941194, t/871484, t/1379886; wiki files "Gift Shop.png" and "Shopping.jpg" (Steal a Brainrot wiki); and the weaker details flagged in rows #3, #48, #25, #43.

- Every card or button in a different saturated colour with no meaning attached, or the opposite: one hue for everything, so nothing is the primary action.
- Chrome, metal and neon-glow gradients; red or coloured text on black; an unreadable display face used for body text.
- Body text and prices that fall under 12 px once the capture is shrunk to phone size; windows drawn for a desktop viewport only.
- Text where a control should be ("(CLICK TO PURCHASE)"), and placeholder blocks or "000 Robux" posted as a design.
- Decoration standing in for content: glow, sunbursts and ice shards round five empty cards; a 3D-tilted panel the player has to read at an angle.
- Sale language with nothing behind it ("20% OFF EVERYTHING!" in a header, "Limited time" with no date).
- Watermarks across the work, and promotional images with the melted look of generated art on a game's own wiki: both end trust on sight.
- Template packs: the same tilted purple panel, green 3D button and seven identical day cards in every game that bought them.
